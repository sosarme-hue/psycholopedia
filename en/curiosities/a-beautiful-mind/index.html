Psychology in film, literature and music

A Beautiful Mind: the real story of John Nash

The Nobel-winning mathematician lived with schizophrenia. The film changed some important details.

John Nash was a brilliant mathematician whose work on game theory earned him the Nobel Prize in Economics in 1994. In the late 1950s he began to have delusions: he believed he was receiving coded messages in newspapers. He was hospitalised several times and diagnosed with schizophrenia.

Film and reality

Ron Howard's film (2001) shows him seeing imaginary people, but Nash said his symptoms were mainly delusional ideas and voices, not visions. It also shows a speech thanking medication for his recovery; in reality, Nash stopped taking medication in the early 1970s and improved gradually, supported by those around him at Princeton.

His case shows that the course of schizophrenia varies and that some people improve greatly over time. But it should not be read as an argument against medication, which reduces relapses for most people.

References

  1. Nasar, S. (1998). A Beautiful Mind. Simon & Schuster.

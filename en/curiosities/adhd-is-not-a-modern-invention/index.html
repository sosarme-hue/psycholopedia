Myths that sound scientific

ADHD is not a modern invention

A Scottish doctor described "mental restlessness" in 1798. That it is diagnosed more today does not mean it did not exist before.

It is often said that attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a recent invention, a product of modern life or the drug industry. History says otherwise.

Early descriptions

In 1798, the Scottish physician Alexander Crichton described a "mental restlessness" that prevented some people from keeping their attention on a task, present from childhood. In 1845, the German psychiatrist Heinrich Hoffmann included Fidgety Philip, a boy who could not sit still at the table, in a children's book. And in 1902 the British paediatrician George Still gave lectures on children with problems of attention and self-control.

It is true that the number of diagnoses has risen and that there is debate about overdiagnosis in some places and underdiagnosis in others, especially in girls and adults. But the difficulties ADHD describes have been recognised for more than two centuries.

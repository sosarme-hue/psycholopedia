Myths that sound scientific

Does alcohol kill brain cells?

Not quite in the way people say, but heavy drinking does damage the brain in other ways.

"Every drink kills thousands of brain cells" is an often-repeated phrase. In 1993, a Danish study counted the neurons in the cerebral cortex of people who had drunk heavily for years and found no fewer neurons than in people who did not drink.

What alcohol does do

Not killing neurons wholesale does not mean it is harmless. Heavy, sustained drinking damages the connections between neurons and the white matter and is associated with reduced brain volume, partly reversible if drinking stops. The vitamin B1 deficiency associated with alcoholism can cause Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, with severe memory problems.

Recent studies also find that even moderate drinking is associated with small changes in the brain. And in adolescence, while the brain is still developing, alcohol carries more risk. The myth was exaggerated, but its underlying message, that heavy drinking harms the brain, is correct.

References

  1. Jensen, G. B. y Pakkenberg, B. (1993). Do alcoholics drink their neurons away? The Lancet, 342(8881), 1201–1204.
  2. Daviet, R. et al. (2022). Associations between alcohol consumption and gray and white matter volumes in the UK Biobank. Nature Communications, 13, 1175.

Myths that sound scientific

Asking about suicide does not cause it

Many people fear "putting the idea in someone's head" by asking. Studies show asking does not increase risk and may help.

When someone close to us is struggling, many people do not dare ask whether they are thinking of ending their life, for fear of giving them the idea. It is an understandable concern, but studies do not support it.

What the evidence says

A 2014 review analysed studies in which adults and adolescents, including some at high risk, were asked directly about suicidal thoughts. Asking did not increase those thoughts and, in some cases, was associated with a slight decrease. Many people feel relief at being able to talk about it.

Another related myth is that "people who talk about it don't do it". In fact, many people who die by suicide had given signs beforehand. If you are worried about someone, ask calmly, listen without judging and help them seek professional support. Please contact a crisis line in your country (in Spain, 024), or emergency services in an emergency.

References

  1. Dazzi, T. et al. (2014). Does asking about suicide and related behaviours induce suicidal ideation? Psychological Medicine, 44(16), 3361–3363.
  2. Organización Mundial de la Salud (2014). Prevención del suicidio: un imperativo global.

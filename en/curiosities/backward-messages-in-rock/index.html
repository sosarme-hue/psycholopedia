Psychology in film, literature and music

Satanic messages played backwards in rock

Playing a record backwards and hearing hidden phrases. The trick lies in what you are told you will hear.

In the 1980s, some religious groups in the United States claimed that songs such as Led Zeppelin's "Stairway to Heaven" contained satanic messages audible only when played backwards. There were hearings in the California legislature and even a 1990 lawsuit against Judas Priest, which the band won.

We hear what we expect

In 1985, psychologists John Vokey and Don Read examined the phenomenon. When listening to recordings played backwards, people only "heard" phrases when someone told them beforehand what to listen for. And even when a message was present, they found no evidence it could be understood or influence behaviour without the listener noticing.

It is a case of auditory pareidolia. Some musicians, such as the Beatles, did include reversed sounds on purpose, but as an artistic device.

References

  1. Vokey, J. R. y Read, J. D. (1985). Subliminal messages: Between the devil and the media. American Psychologist, 40(11), 1231–1239.

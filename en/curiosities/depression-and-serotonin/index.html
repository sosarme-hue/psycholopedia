Myths that sound scientific

Is depression a lack of serotonin?

The "chemical imbalance" idea was a simplification. That does not mean antidepressants do not work.

In the 1990s the idea spread that depression is caused by a "chemical imbalance", specifically a lack of serotonin, and that antidepressants correct it. It was an easy message to grasp and widely used in drug advertising.

What the studies say

Researchers had long warned that it was a simplification. In 2022, a review by Joanna Moncrieff and colleagues found no consistent evidence that people with depression have low serotonin levels. Depression is a complex problem shaped by genetics, stress, life circumstances and ways of thinking.

That does not mean antidepressants do not work. Large reviews show that, overall, they are more effective than placebo, especially in moderate or severe depression, although it is not fully understood why. If you take one, do not stop it suddenly: talk to your doctor first.

References

  1. Moncrieff, J. et al. (2022). The serotonin theory of depression: a systematic umbrella review of the evidence. Molecular Psychiatry, 28, 3243–3256.
  2. Cipriani, A. et al. (2018). Comparative efficacy and acceptability of 21 antidepressant drugs. The Lancet, 391(10128), 1357–1366.

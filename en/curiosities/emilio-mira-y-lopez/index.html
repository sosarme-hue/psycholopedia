Forgotten figures

Emilio Mira y López and the four giants of the soul

Psychiatrist to the Republican army, inventor of a test of lines drawn blind and a key figure of psychology in Brazil.

Emilio Mira y López (1896–1964) was born in Santiago de Cuba and trained in Barcelona. He directed the Institute of Vocational Guidance and in 1933 took up the first chair of psychiatry at the University of Barcelona. During the Spanish Civil War he headed the psychiatric services of the Republican army.

A test with eyes closed

In exile, in London in 1939, he presented the myokinetic psychodiagnosis (PMK): the person draws lines and shapes without seeing what they are doing, and deviations in the strokes were interpreted as traits of temperament. It was widely used in Latin America and Spain, although its validity is now considered limited.

He settled in Brazil, where he directed the Institute of Vocational Selection and Guidance in Rio de Janeiro and helped psychology become recognised as a profession. His popular book Four Giants of the Soul (1947), on fear, anger, love and duty, is still in print.

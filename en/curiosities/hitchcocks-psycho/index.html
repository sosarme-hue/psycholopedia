Psychology in film, literature and music

Hitchcock's Psycho: what the film gets mixed up

A suspense masterpiece that mixed up psychosis, dissociated identity and violence, and left a lasting stigma.

In 1960, Alfred Hitchcock released Psycho, based on a Robert Bloch novel itself inspired by the killer Ed Gein. At the end, a psychiatrist explains that Norman Bates had "become" his dead mother.

What it mixes up

The title refers to psychosis, but what it describes is closer to dissociative identity disorder, a different and controversial diagnosis. And it presents both as synonyms for danger. In reality, most people with psychosis are not violent, and they are more likely to be victims of violence than perpetrators.

The film is a landmark of cinema, and its shower scene transformed horror. But it also contributed to the image of the "psycho" with a mental disorder, an association that stigma studies still find among the public.

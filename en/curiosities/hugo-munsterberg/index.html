Forgotten figures

Hugo Münsterberg: witnesses, factories and film

He pioneered forensic psychology, work psychology and even film theory. Then he was forgotten.

Hugo Münsterberg (1863–1916) was German and had studied with Wundt when William James invited him to run Harvard's psychology laboratory in 1892. He wanted psychology to address practical problems.

Three new fields

In On the Witness Stand (1908) he warned that witnesses make mistakes and that memory is swayed by questioning, decades before Loftus's studies. In Psychology and Industrial Efficiency (1913) he applied psychological tests to selecting workers. And in The Photoplay (1916) he analysed how cinema imitates attention, memory and imagination through the close-up or the flashback.

He died suddenly in 1916 while giving a lecture. In the midst of the First World War, his defence of Germany had made him very unpopular in the United States, and his work was forgotten for years.

References

  2. Münsterberg, H. (1916). The Photoplay: A Psychological Study. Appleton.

Myths that sound scientific

"Hypnosis recovers forgotten memories"

Hypnosis increases confidence in what is remembered, but not its accuracy.

In films and TV series, hypnosis lets a witness recall a number plate or a patient relive a forgotten childhood trauma. In the 1980s and 1990s it was used in some countries in police investigations and in therapy.

More memories, not better ones

Studies show that hypnotised people recall more, but both true and false details, and they become more confident about all of it. They are also more susceptible to the questioner's suggestions. In "recovered memory" therapy this led to accusations of abuse that never happened.

That is why many courts do not admit testimony obtained under hypnosis. Hypnosis does have clinical uses with scientific support, for example as an add-on in treating pain or irritable bowel syndrome, but not as a way to open a hidden memory archive.

References

  1. Lynn, S. J., Lock, T., Loftus, E. F., Krackow, E. y Lilienfeld, S. O. (2003). The remembrance of things past. En S. O. Lilienfeld et al. (eds.), Science and Pseudoscience in Clinical Psychology. Guilford.
  2. Steblay, N. M. y Bothwell, R. K. (1994). Evidence for hypnotically refreshed testimony. Law and Human Behavior, 18(6), 635–651.

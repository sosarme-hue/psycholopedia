Forgotten figures

Inez Beverly Prosser and the education of Black children

The first African American woman with a PhD in psychology studied in 1933 whether school segregation helped or harmed.

Inez Beverly Prosser was born in Texas around 1895 into a family that placed great value on education. She worked as a teacher for years and in 1933 earned her doctorate at the University of Cincinnati. She is regarded as the first African American woman with a PhD in psychology in the United States.

Separate or mixed schools?

Her thesis compared African American children attending all-Black schools with those attending mixed schools. She found the former felt more accepted and were better adjusted socially, and concluded that in a racist society segregated schools could protect children. It was a controversial position that would later contrast with the Clarks' doll study.

Prosser died in 1934, aged 38, in a car accident, barely a year after earning her doctorate. Her work has been rediscovered in recent decades.

References

  1. Benjamin, L. T., Henry, K. D. y McMahon, L. R. (2005). Inez Beverly Prosser and the education of African Americans. Journal of the History of the Behavioral Sciences, 41(1), 43–62.

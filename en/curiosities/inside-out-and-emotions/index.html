Psychology in film, literature and music

Inside Out and the basic emotions

Pixar consulted psychologists to bring Joy, Sadness, Fear, Anger and Disgust to life. And it got something important right.

For Inside Out (2015), director Pete Docter consulted psychologists Paul Ekman and Dacher Keltner. Ekman had proposed a set of basic emotions recognisable across cultures; the writers chose five of them to be characters.

Sadness has a purpose too

The film's central message is that sadness has a function: it lets us ask for help and be comforted, and it helps us process loss. Riley gets better when she stops pretending everything is fine and says how she feels.

There are simplifications: emotions do not live in separate characters, memories are not glass balls with a fixed colour, and the idea of a fixed number of basic emotions is debated today. But the film is right that emotions are not good or bad, but useful depending on the context.

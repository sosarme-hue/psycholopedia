Forgotten figures

Karen Horney, the psychoanalyst who answered Freud

She rejected "penis envy" and suggested men might envy the ability to give birth. She stressed the weight of culture.

Karen Horney (1885–1952) was one of the first women to study medicine in Germany and trained as a psychoanalyst in Berlin. She soon disagreed with Freud on a central point: his view of female psychology.

Womb envy

Freud held that girls felt "penis envy". Horney replied that if women envied anything about men, it was their power and opportunities in society, not their anatomy. And she suggested, with some irony, that men might feel womb envy, of the ability to bear children, and compensate with achievements in other areas.

In 1932 she settled in the United States. She argued that culture and relationships weigh more than instincts in personality, described "neurotic needs" and wrote Self-Analysis (1942), one of the first books to encourage people to explore themselves. She was expelled from the New York psychoanalytic institute for her views and founded her own.

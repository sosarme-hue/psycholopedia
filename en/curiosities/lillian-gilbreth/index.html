Forgotten figures

Lillian Gilbreth, twelve children and the modern kitchen

A psychologist and engineer, she studied how to work with less effort. Two of her children told her story in Cheaper by the Dozen.

Lillian Moller Gilbreth (1878–1972) earned a doctorate in psychology and, with her husband Frank, studied workers' movements to eliminate unnecessary effort. Unlike other efficiency experts of the time, she stressed people's wellbeing and motivation, and published The Psychology of Management (1914).

A famous family

They had twelve children. Two of them wrote Cheaper by the Dozen (1948), a humorous portrait of a household run like a factory, which was made into a film in 1950.

When Frank died in 1924, Lillian carried on alone with both the consultancy and the family. She designed kitchens that saved steps and is credited with ideas such as shelves in the refrigerator door and the pedal bin. She was the first woman elected to the US National Academy of Engineering.

The story behind an idea

Little Hans and the fear of horses

Freud interpreted a five-year-old's phobia as an Oedipal conflict. Behaviourists proposed a simpler explanation.

In 1909, Sigmund Freud published the case of a five-year-old boy with an intense fear of horses, especially of being bitten or of them falling down in the street. Freud saw the boy only once: the treatment was carried out by his father, the music critic Max Graf, who sent him detailed notes.

Two readings

Freud interpreted the horse as representing the father, and the fear as reflecting the Oedipus complex: the boy desired his mother and feared his father's punishment. It was the first published analysis of a child and a key case for child psychoanalysis.

In 1960, the behaviourists Joseph Wolpe and Stanley Rachman pointed to a detail in the account itself: the phobia began after the boy saw a horse pulling a cart fall down. For them it was a fear learned through conditioning. The boy, Herbert Graf, grew up without problems and became an opera stage director in New York.

References

  1. Freud, S. (1909). Analyse der Phobie eines fünfjährigen Knaben. Jahrbuch für psychoanalytische und psychopathologische Forschungen, 1, 1–109.
  2. Wolpe, J. y Rachman, S. (1960). Psychoanalytic 'evidence': a critique based on Freud's case of Little Hans. Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease, 131(2), 135–148.

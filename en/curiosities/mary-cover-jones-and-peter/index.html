Forgotten figures

Mary Cover Jones, the mother of behaviour therapy

In 1924 she helped a boy lose his fear of rabbits. It was the first step towards graded exposure.

Shortly after the Little Albert case, Mary Cover Jones wondered whether a learned fear could be unlearned. She worked with Peter, a boy of almost three who feared rabbits. While Peter ate something he liked, the rabbit was brought a little closer, day after day.

Step by step

In the end Peter let the rabbit climb onto his lap. Jones also found that it helped for him to see other children playing calmly with the animal. Today we would call this graded exposure and observational learning.

Her work was overlooked for decades. Joseph Wolpe, who developed systematic desensitisation in the 1950s, acknowledged her as a pioneer. Exposure therapies are now among the best-supported treatments for phobias.

References

  1. Jones, M. C. (1924). A laboratory study of fear: The case of Peter. Pedagogical Seminary, 31(4), 308–315.

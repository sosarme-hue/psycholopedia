Myths that sound scientific

Memory is not a recording

Remembering is rebuilding. That is why we can be sure of something that never happened.

Many people believe memory works like a video camera: everything is stored and we only need to find the recording. Surveys in the United States show more than half the population thinks so.

Remembering is rebuilding

As early as 1932, Frederic Bartlett asked British students to recall a Native American legend, "The War of the Ghosts". With each retelling, the story became shorter and was adapted to what felt familiar to them. Decades later, Elizabeth Loftus showed that a question worded in a certain way can change what a witness believes they saw.

Remembering means rebuilding the memory each time, filling gaps with what we expect. How confident we feel about a memory does not guarantee it is accurate. That is why witnesses are now interviewed without leading questions.

References

  1. Bartlett, F. C. (1932). Remembering. Cambridge University Press.
  2. Simons, D. J. y Chabris, C. F. (2011). What people believe about how memory works. PLoS ONE, 6(8), e22757.

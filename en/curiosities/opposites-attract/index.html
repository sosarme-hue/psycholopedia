Myths that sound scientific

"Opposites attract"

In love and friendship the reverse is almost always true: we are drawn to people like us.

It is a familiar plot in romantic comedies: two very different people who end up in love. The idea is appealing because it promises that someone will complete us.

What the studies show

Since Donn Byrne's work in the 1960s, research has shown the opposite: we tend to be attracted to people with similar attitudes, values, education and interests. This is called homophily. A 2008 meta-analysis confirmed that perceived similarity predicts attraction.

In personality, similarity matters somewhat less, and there is no evidence that couples with opposite traits do better. What does help is complementarity in specific tasks: one person enjoys cooking and the other does not mind washing up.

References

  1. Byrne, D. (1971). The Attraction Paradigm. Academic Press.
  2. Montoya, R. M., Horton, R. S. y Kirchner, J. (2008). Is actual similarity necessary for attraction? Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 25(6), 889–922.

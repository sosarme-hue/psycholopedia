What the original study did not say

Did Pavlov really use a bell?

The dog drooling at a bell is a famous image. The records mostly mention metronomes and buzzers.

Ivan Pavlov won the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 1904 for his studies of digestion, not for conditioning. While measuring dogs' saliva, he noticed they began to salivate before receiving food: when they saw the assistant or heard his footsteps. He called these responses "conditional reflexes".

The bell of legend

In his experiments he used metronomes, buzzers, lights, tuning forks and touch. A bell appears in some accounts, and historians still debate whether he ever used one, but it was not the main stimulus. The image spread through textbooks and cartoons.

What matters is the finding: a neutral stimulus repeatedly paired with one that triggers a response eventually triggers it on its own. Pavlov also described extinction (the response fades if the stimulus stops signalling food) and generalisation to similar stimuli.

References

  1. Pavlov, I. P. (1927). Conditioned Reflexes. Oxford University Press.
  2. Todes, D. P. (2014). Ivan Pavlov: A Russian Life in Science. Oxford University Press.

Psychology in film, literature and music

Rain Man and the real Kim Peek

Dustin Hoffman's character was inspired by a man with a prodigious memory. His story was different.

Rain Man (1988) tells the story of Raymond, an autistic man with extraordinary skills in calculation and memory. The screenwriter Barry Morrow was inspired by Kim Peek, whom he met in 1984.

Kim Peek

Peek memorised about 12,000 books, read two pages at once, one with each eye, and remembered facts about geography, history and music. But he was not autistic: he was born with brain malformations, including an absent corpus callosum, and had difficulties with movement and daily living. After the film he travelled giving talks with his father.

The film raised awareness of autism, but it also spread the idea that all autistic people have a "special talent". Savant syndrome exists but is uncommon: it is estimated to occur in about one in ten autistic people, to very different degrees.

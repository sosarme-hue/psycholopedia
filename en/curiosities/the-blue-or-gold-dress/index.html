Your perception surprises you

The dress: blue and black or white and gold?

In 2015 a photo split the internet. It showed that each brain decides differently what light is falling on a scene.

In February 2015, a photo of a dress went viral. Some people saw it as blue with black lace, others as white with gold. The real dress was blue and black.

A matter of assumed light

To see colour, the brain discounts the colour of the light falling on objects: that is why a sheet of paper looks white both in sunlight and under a bulb. The photo was ambiguous. If the brain assumed the dress was in shadow, under bluish light, it "subtracted" blue and saw white and gold. If it assumed yellowish artificial light, it saw blue and black.

Later research found that people who spend more time in daylight, such as early risers, tended to see it as white and gold. It was a reminder that colour is not just in the object but in the brain's interpretation.

References

  1. Wallisch, P. (2017). Illumination assumptions account for individual differences in the perceptual interpretation of a profoundly ambiguous stimulus in the color domain. Journal of Vision, 17(4), 5.
  2. Brainard, D. H. y Hurlbert, A. C. (2015). Colour vision: Understanding #TheDress. Current Biology, 25(13), R551–R554.

Your perception surprises you

The hollow-face illusion

You look at the inside of a mask and see it standing out towards you.

If you turn a mask round and look at its concave inside, from a certain distance it will look convex: the nose seems to come towards you. If the mask rotates, it seems to follow you with its eyes.

Knowledge beats the eyes

The brain has a very strong expectation: faces stick out. That expectation overrides the depth cues coming from the eyes, such as shading or the difference between what each eye sees. The psychologist Richard Gregory used it as an example of perception being the brain's hypothesis about the world.

Some studies have found that certain people with schizophrenia are less susceptible to the illusion, suggesting differences in how they combine expectations and sensory data. Fairground attractions use it to make statues that "watch you".

References

  1. Gregory, R. L. (1970). The Intelligent Eye. Weidenfeld & Nicolson.
  2. Dima, D. et al. (2009). Understanding why patients with schizophrenia do not perceive the hollow-mask illusion. NeuroImage, 46(4), 1180–1186.

Experiments that changed questions

The Jim twins: separated at birth

Two twins adopted by different families met again at 39 with an astonishing list of coincidences.

In 1979, two men from Ohio met for the first time at the age of 39. They were identical twins, separated a few weeks after birth and adopted by different families. Both families had named them Jim.

Coincidences and science

The list of similarities became famous: both had married a Linda, divorced and remarried a Betty, had a son with almost the same name and had owned a dog called Toy. They smoked the same brand and enjoyed carpentry.

Their case spurred the Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart, led by Thomas Bouchard, which assessed dozens of pairs. The striking anecdotes are partly chance: look for coincidences between any two people and you will always find some. But the systematic study found that identical twins raised apart were very alike in intelligence, personality and interests, pointing to an important genetic influence.

References

  1. Bouchard, T. J. et al. (1990). Sources of human psychological differences: the Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart. Science, 250(4978), 223–228.
  2. Segal, N. L. (2012). Born Together, Reared Apart. Harvard University Press.

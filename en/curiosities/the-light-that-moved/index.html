Experiments that changed questions

The light that moved: how group norms are born

A still point of light in a dark room seems to move. Sherif used it to see how a group creates its own rules.

If you stare at a fixed point of light in a completely dark room, after a while it seems to move. This illusion is called the autokinetic effect and results from small eye movements with no surrounding reference.

From individual judgement to norm

In 1935, Muzafer Sherif asked people to estimate how far the light moved. Alone, each gave different figures. When they repeated the task in a group, out loud, their estimates converged on a common value. And when they later did it alone again, they kept using the group's figure.

Sherif showed how social norms arise in uncertain situations: when we do not know what is right, we take others as a reference, and that reference stays with us. It underlies many fashions, customs and shared opinions.

References

  1. Sherif, M. (1935). A study of some social factors in perception. Archives of Psychology, 27(187).

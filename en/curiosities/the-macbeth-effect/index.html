What the original study did not say

The Macbeth effect: does washing your hands clear your conscience?

A 2006 study in Science said recalling a bad deed makes people want to wash. Replications did not find it.

In Shakespeare's Macbeth, Lady Macbeth rubs her hands trying to erase an imaginary bloodstain: "Out, damned spot!". In 2006, Chen-Bo Zhong and Katie Liljenquist reported in Science that a similar effect existed in real life.

What was claimed

People who recalled something unethical they had done chose an antiseptic wipe as a gift more often than those who recalled a good deed, and after washing their hands they felt less guilty. They called it the Macbeth effect, and it became an example of how the physical and the moral intertwine.

But several teams repeated it with larger samples and did not find the effect. Today it is regarded as another case of the replication crisis. What is well documented is that disgust and moral judgement are related, though the link is more modest than the headlines suggested.

References

  1. Zhong, C.-B. y Liljenquist, K. (2006). Washing away your sins. Science, 313(5792), 1451–1452.
  2. Earp, B. D. et al. (2014). Out, damned spot: can the 'Macbeth effect' be replicated? Basic and Applied Social Psychology, 36(1), 91–98.

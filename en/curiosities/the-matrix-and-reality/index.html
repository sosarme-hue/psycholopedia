Psychology in film, literature and music

The Matrix and Descartes's question

What if everything you perceive were a simulation? The film revives an old philosophical problem that neuroscience sees differently.

In The Matrix (1999), Neo discovers that the world he lives in is a simulation created by machines and that his real body lies in a pod. The idea is not new: in 1641, René Descartes asked in his Meditations how he could be sure an "evil demon" was not deceiving him about everything he perceived. In 1981, the philosopher Hilary Putnam updated it with a "brain in a vat" connected to a computer.

What neuroscience says

The psychology of perception offers a less dramatic but unsettling version: what we see is not a direct copy of the world but a model that the brain builds from incomplete signals and its expectations. Visual illusions, the blind spot and dreams show this.

Some neuroscientists, such as Anil Seth, describe perception as a "controlled hallucination": the brain proposes hypotheses and corrects them with what comes in through the senses. We do not live in the Matrix, but we do live in an interpreted reality.

  2. Putnam, H. (1981). Reason, Truth and History. Cambridge University Press.

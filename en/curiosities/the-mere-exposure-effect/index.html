Experiments that changed questions

We like what we have seen before

Made-up words, Chinese characters or songs: familiarity breeds liking.

In 1968, Robert Zajonc showed American students made-up words said to be Turkish, and Chinese characters. Some appeared once and others up to 25 times. He then asked whether they thought each one meant something good or bad. The more often they had seen a word, the more positive it seemed.

Familiar feels safe

He called it the mere exposure effect. It works even when images are shown so briefly that people do not remember seeing them. One explanation is that familiar things are easier to process, and that ease is read as liking or safety.

That is why we like a song more after hearing it a few times, and why advertisers repeat their ads. There is a limit: too much exposure brings boredom, and if we dislike something to begin with, repetition does not help.

References

  1. Zajonc, R. B. (1968). Attitudinal effects of mere exposure. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 9(2), 1–27.
  2. Bornstein, R. F. (1989). Exposure and affect. Psychological Bulletin, 106(2), 265–289.

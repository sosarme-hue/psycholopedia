Your perception surprises you

The giant moon illusion

The moon looks huge near the horizon, but it takes up the same space on the retina. After two thousand years, why is still debated.

When the full moon rises over the horizon, it looks much larger than when it is high in the sky. But if you photograph it or measure it with an outstretched finger, its size is the same; in fact, at the horizon it is slightly farther away and a little smaller.

A very old illusion

Ptolemy discussed it as early as the 2nd century. A classic explanation is apparent distance: the sky looks to us like a flattened dome, and the horizon, with buildings and mountains, seems farther away than the zenith. If an object takes up the same space on the retina but we think it is farther away, the brain "enlarges" it.

Another explanation is based on contrast: next to trees or rooftops, the moon looks big; alone in a vast sky, small. Several factors are probably involved. A trick to check it is an illusion: look at the horizon moon bending down, between your legs, without seeing the landscape. It usually looks smaller.

Experiments that changed questions

The pratfall effect: a slip makes you more likeable

A highly competent person who spilled coffee on themselves was liked more. If they were not competent, they were liked less.

In 1966, Elliot Aronson and colleagues asked students to listen to a recording of a candidate for a quiz team. In one version he answered almost everything correctly and mentioned he had been brilliant at school. In another, he got few right and had an ordinary record.

The spilled coffee

At the end of some recordings, the candidate could be heard spilling a cup of coffee over his suit. When the brilliant candidate made the clumsy mistake, listeners rated him as more likeable than when he did not. When the mediocre candidate made it, they rated him lower.

Aronson called it the pratfall effect: in a highly competent person, a small mistake makes them seem more human and approachable. Later studies showed the effect depends on who is watching and how serious the error is. The practical lesson: there is no need to seem perfect; a minor slip, acknowledged naturally, does not detract and may help.

References

  1. Aronson, E., Willerman, B. y Floyd, J. (1966). The effect of a pratfall on increasing interpersonal attractiveness. Psychonomic Science, 4(6), 227–228.

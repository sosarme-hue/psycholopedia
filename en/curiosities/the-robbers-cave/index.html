Experiments that changed questions

Robbers Cave: rivalry and cooperation

Two groups of boys at a summer camp, a competition and a way to reconcile them.

In 1954, Muzafer Sherif ran a summer camp at Robbers Cave State Park in Oklahoma, with boys of about eleven split into two groups that did not know each other. Each group chose a name: the Eagles and the Rattlers.

From rivalry to cooperation

When the researchers set up competitions with prizes, name-calling, flag burning and cabin raids followed. Simply bringing them together for pleasant activities was not enough. What reduced the hostility were superordinate goals: problems they could solve only together, such as fixing the water supply or pushing a broken-down truck.

The historian Gina Perry has shown that an earlier attempt, in 1953, failed because the boys refused to turn on each other and suspected the counsellors. Even so, the idea of shared goals still inspires programmes to reduce prejudice.

References

  1. Sherif, M., Harvey, O. J., White, B. J., Hood, W. R. y Sherif, C. W. (1961). Intergroup Conflict and Cooperation: The Robbers Cave Experiment. University of Oklahoma.
  2. Perry, G. (2018). The Lost Boys. Scribe.

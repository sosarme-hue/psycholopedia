Psychology in film, literature and music

Van Gogh and his illness: more than thirty diagnoses

He has been credited with epilepsy, bipolar disorder, absinthe poisoning and much more. He painted despite his suffering, not because of it.

In December 1888, in Arles, Vincent van Gogh cut off part of his left ear after a crisis. In the following months he had further episodes, and in 1889 he voluntarily entered the asylum at Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, where he painted around 150 pictures, including The Starry Night. He died in July 1890.

An impossible diagnosis

Doctors and historians have credited him with more than thirty diagnoses: epilepsy, bipolar disorder, borderline personality disorder, porphyria, Ménière's disease, or poisoning from absinthe or paint. In 2016, a symposium at the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam concluded that several factors were probably involved, including alcohol use, poor sleep and diet, and stress.

His letters to his brother Theo show a lucid man who thought deeply about his art. He himself wrote that the crises stopped him working. The romantic idea of the genius who creates thanks to his madness does not fit his story: he painted during the periods when he was better.

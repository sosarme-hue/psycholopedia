Experiments that changed questions

Wason's four cards

A logic problem almost everyone gets wrong… unless it is framed as a social rule.

In 1966, the psychologist Peter Wason posed a problem. On the table are four cards showing E, K, 4 and 7. Each card has a letter on one side and a number on the other. The rule is: "If a card has a vowel on one side, it has an even number on the other." Which cards must you turn over to check whether the rule holds?

What we get wrong

Most people choose E, or E and 4. The correct answer is E and 7: if there were a vowel behind the 7, the rule would be false. The 4 is no use, because the rule says nothing about what is behind an even number. Fewer than 10% usually get it right.

But if the same problem is framed like this: "If someone drinks beer, they must be over 18", with cards reading "beer", "soft drink", "25 years old" and "16 years old", almost everyone gets it right: you must check the beer and the 16-year-old. We reason much better when the problem is about rules and catching cheats.

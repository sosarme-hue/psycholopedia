Decisions

Why do I buy things I don't need?

Impulse purchases respond to emotions and to an environment designed to tempt us. Small pauses help a lot.

You went in for bread and came out with three other things. You saw a "today only" offer and bought without thinking. Later, at home, you wonder why you wanted it.

Emotion before reason

In 2001, Bas Verplanken and Astrid Herabadi described impulse buying with two components: an emotional one, the sudden urge and excitement of buying, and a cognitive one, the lack of reflection and planning. More impulsive people buy more often to lift their mood, and the relief is short-lived.

An environment designed to tempt

Shops and websites exploit our mental shortcuts: countdown offers, "last few items", one-click buying, products placed next to the till and personalised recommendations. Paying by card or phone also hurts less than paying cash.

A useful right

In the European Union, most distance purchases, online or by phone, can be returned within 14 days without giving a reason. This is the right of withdrawal, set out in a 2011 European directive. It does not apply to all purchases made in physical shops.

When shopping becomes a problem

For a minority, shopping stops being an occasional treat and becomes hard to control, with debts, arguments and hidden purchases. A meta-analysis by Aniko Maraz estimated that around 5% of adults have compulsive buying. It is often used to relieve sadness, anxiety or emptiness, and the relief is short-lived. Cognitive behavioural therapy has shown promising results, and it is worth seeking help if shopping is affecting finances or relationships.

Experiences rather than things

Leaf Van Boven and Thomas Gilovich found that, in general, people felt happier with experiential purchases, such as a trip, a concert or dinner with friends, than with material ones. Experiences are shared, become part of who we are and are remembered ever more fondly, whereas we get used to objects and compare them with others. Before an impulse buy, it may help to ask what experience you could have with that money.

What usually helps

  • The 24-hour rule: leave it in the basket and decide the next day.
  • Go with a list and, if you can, on a full stomach.
  • Delete saved cards from online shops.
  • Unsubscribe from offer newsletters.
  • Ask yourself what emotion lies behind it: boredom, sadness, stress.
If shopping causes debts or problems you cannot control, seek help: compulsive buying disorder can be treated.

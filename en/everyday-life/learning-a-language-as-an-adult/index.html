Study

Is it too late to learn a language as an adult?

Children have the edge in sounding native, but adults learn fast at first and can reach a very high level.

"Children learn languages like sponges; as an adult it's too late" is a widespread idea that discourages many people. The reality is more nuanced.

What studies say

In 2018, Joshua Hartshorne, Joshua Tenenbaum and Steven Pinker analysed an English grammar test taken by nearly 670,000 people. They found that the ability to master grammar like a native stays high until about age 17 and then declines. To reach native level, it helped to start before 10.

But that refers to sounding exactly like a native. Adults learn faster at the start: they have more vocabulary, study strategies and the ability to reason about rules. Many people who start as adults reach a very high level, enough to work, study or live in another language. Accent is the hardest thing to lose, and it does not prevent communication.

Adults start faster

The fact that children end up reaching a more native-like level does not mean they learn faster. In a classic study of English speakers who moved to the Netherlands, Catherine Snow and Marian Hoefnagel-Höhle found that in the first months, adolescents and adults progressed faster than young children in vocabulary and grammar. Adults have on their side the ability to reason about rules, experience with their own language and motivation.

Does bilingualism protect the brain?

Ellen Bialystok and colleagues found that lifelong bilinguals began to show symptoms of dementia about four years later than monolinguals. The idea that using two languages trains attentional control has proved controversial: other studies have not found these advantages or attribute them to differences in education or background. What is clear is that learning a language is a stimulating and social activity at any age.

What usually helps

  • Plenty of exposure: series, podcasts, reading and real conversations.
  • Practise recall: trying to say or write without looking helps more than rereading.
  • Review at spaced intervals, with apps or flashcards.
  • Speak early, even with mistakes.
  • Find your own reasons: travel, a series, a person. Motivation sustains effort.

What is more, learning languages is good mental exercise at any age.

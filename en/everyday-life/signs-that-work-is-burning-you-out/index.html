Work

Signs that work is burning you out

Exhaustion, cynicism and a sense of being useless. Spotting it early lets you act before you crash.

You wake up tired even after sleeping, what you used to enjoy about your job now irritates you, and you feel that whatever you do, it makes no difference. It may be more than just a bad patch.

The three signs

The psychologist Christina Maslach, who has studied burnout for decades, describes it with three dimensions:

  • Exhaustion: feeling empty and drained, physically and emotionally.
  • Detachment or cynicism: treating your work, or the people you serve, with coldness, indifference or irritation.
  • Inefficacy: feeling that you are not performing, achieving anything or competent.

The World Health Organization has recognised it since 2019 as an occupational phenomenon, caused by chronic workplace stress that has not been well managed.

What causes it

Maslach and Michael Leiter point to six areas of the organisation that matter: excessive workload, lack of control over your own tasks, little recognition, poor relationships with colleagues, a sense of unfairness and a clash between personal and company values. That is why it is not solved by relaxation techniques alone.

Burnout or depression?

Burnout and depression look very alike: tiredness, lack of energy, low mood and difficulty concentrating. Renzo Bianchi and colleagues reviewed the studies and found such a large overlap that in many cases what is called burnout meets the criteria for depression. The practical difference is that depression affects every area of life, not only work. If the distress no longer improves on holiday or at weekends, it is worth seeing a doctor.

Demands and resources

The job demands-resources model, by Wilmar Schaufeli and Arnold Bakker, explains that burnout appears when demands are high and resources are scarce. Resources include autonomy, support from colleagues and managers, recognition and opportunities to learn. When they are plentiful, people not only burn out less: they also feel more engaged and energised. That is why the most effective solutions are usually organisational, not only individual.

What usually helps

  • Identify which areas are failing and raise it with someone who can change things.
  • Protect your rest and switch off outside working hours.
  • Lean on colleagues, who often feel the same.
  • Reconnect with what gives your work meaning, even if it is a small part.
If exhaustion stops you functioning or comes with persistent sadness, insomnia or anxiety, see your doctor. Sick leave and treatment may be needed.

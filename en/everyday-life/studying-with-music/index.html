Study

Does studying with music help?

It depends on the task and the music. Songs with lyrics tend to hurt reading; soft music sometimes does no harm.

Many people study with headphones on. Some say music helps them concentrate; others cannot study with any noise at all. What do studies say?

Lyrics distract

The most replicated finding is that music with lyrics harms verbal tasks, such as reading or memorising texts. In 2014, Nick Perham and Harriet Currie asked students to read texts in silence, with music without lyrics or with music with lyrics. Performance was worse with lyrics, whether or not they liked the music. The words of the song compete with those of the text for the same mental resources.

Volume and tempo matter too. In 2012, a study found that fast, loud music worsened reading comprehension, while slow, soft music had little effect.

When it can help

Listening to music before studying can lift mood and arousal, which helps you get going. And for repetitive or undemanding tasks, such as organising notes, it can make the work more bearable. For some people, a steady background helps mask more annoying noises.

It depends on the person

Not everyone is affected in the same way. Adrian Furnham and Anna Bradley found that background music impaired the performance of introverts more than that of extraverts on memory and reading comprehension tasks. One explanation is that introverts already have a higher level of arousal and the extra stimulation is too much. Habit matters too: someone who always studies with music may notice it less.

The "Mozart effect" myth

In 1993, a short study published in Nature found that listening to a Mozart sonata improved performance on a spatial task for a few minutes. The idea was popularised as "Mozart makes babies smarter". A meta-analysis by Jakob Pietschnig of about forty studies concluded that the effect is small, inconsistent and not specific to Mozart: it is better explained by the good mood and arousal produced by any pleasant music.

What usually helps

  • Silence or soft instrumental music for reading, memorising or solving hard problems.
  • Save music with lyrics for mechanical tasks or breaks.
  • Test and compare: quizzing yourself on what you studied under both conditions gives a more reliable answer than your impression.

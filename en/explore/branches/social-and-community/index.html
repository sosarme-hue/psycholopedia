Psychology: social and community

Participation, exclusion, support networks and living together.

What it does

Social and community psychology works with groups, neighbourhoods and communities rather than isolated individuals. It seeks to understand how social conditions (poverty, isolation, discrimination, lack of resources) affect well-being, and to design interventions together with the people themselves to improve their lives. It emphasises prevention, participation and empowerment.

Where it works

  • Municipal social services and inclusion programmes.
  • Support for migrants, refugees and homeless people.
  • Prevention of gender-based violence and drug use.
  • Neighbourhood associations, NGOs, international cooperation and emergencies.

What research has contributed

  • Loneliness and isolation are associated with poorer health and higher mortality, with an effect comparable to other well-known risk factors.
  • Social support protects against stress and helps people recover from crises.
  • Contact between groups, on an equal footing and with shared goals, reduces prejudice (Gordon Allport, 1954).
  • Psychological first aid: after a disaster, what helps is safety, calm, connection and practical help, not forcing people to recount what happened.

An example

The Housing First model offers homeless people with mental health problems a stable home without first requiring them to stop using substances or follow treatment. Trials in Canada and other countries have shown that it keeps many more people housed than staircase models. In Spain it is applied by programmes such as Hábitat, run by the organisation HOGAR SÍ.

Roots

Community psychology took shape in the United States after the Swampscott conference in 1965, and developed its own path in Latin America with the liberation psychology of Ignacio Martín-Baró, a Spanish psychologist and Jesuit murdered in El Salvador in 1989.

What to know about antidepressants

They take time to work, do not change your personality and should not be stopped suddenly. The most common questions, answered calmly.

By Alejandro Rosàs, family doctor

When an antidepressant is suggested in the surgery, the same questions almost always come up. They are worth answering, because fear often leads people to stop treatment before it has had a chance to work.

"Will I get hooked?"

Antidepressants are not addictive in the way drugs are: they do not create cravings and the dose does not have to be increased to get the same effect. They can, however, cause symptoms when stopped abruptly, such as dizziness, irritability, "electric shock" sensations or insomnia. That is why they are tapered gradually, as agreed with the doctor.

"I don't feel anything"

The effect on mood usually takes two to four weeks to be noticed, and some side effects, such as nausea or jitteriness, can appear earlier and fade within a few days. It is important not to give up at this stage and to mention side effects: adjustments are often possible.

"Will they change me?"

They do not change your personality or make life look rosy. Their aim is to help the person get back to their usual self and be able to deal with what is happening. Some people notice a certain emotional flatness; if that happens, it is worth mentioning.

"For how long?"

Guidelines usually recommend continuing treatment for at least six months after feeling well, to reduce the risk of relapse. After that, stopping is considered.

"What about therapy?"

In mild depression, psychotherapy and other measures are usually the first option. In moderate and severe depression, combining psychotherapy and medication works better than either alone. They are not opposite paths.

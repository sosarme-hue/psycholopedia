Hard times

Life's hardest moments

The problems that affect us most, explained with what research knows: what happens, what is normal and what tends to help.

Some experiences come to almost everyone at some point and shake the whole of life: losing someone, losing a job, separating, feeling alone or out of place. They are not illnesses but part of life, yet they can hurt a great deal and sometimes lead to mental health problems if they pile up or drag on.

In 1967, the psychiatrists Thomas Holmes and Richard Rahe asked hundreds of people to rate how much readjustment different events required. At the top were the death of a spouse, divorce, separation, the death of a close family member and losing one's job. Their scale has limitations, because each person experiences things differently, but it showed something now well established: major life changes affect the health of body and mind.

In this section we go through the hard times that affect us most, what research says about each and what tends to help. They are no substitute for seeing a professional.

The moments we cover

  1. Losing your job: Unemployment takes away more than income: it also removes structure, contacts and purpose. What we know about its impact and what helps.
  2. Divorce and separation: Separating is one of the most stressful life changes. How it usually unfolds, what affects children and what helps you rebuild.
  3. Moving home: Moving house or city seems a practical matter, but it breaks routines and ties. Why it is hard and how to make it easier.
  4. Feeling that you don't stand out: Comparing yourself with the best is a recipe for feeling small. Why it happens and how to measure life by a different yardstick.
  5. Not doing what you really love: Feeling that life is slipping away on something that does not fulfil you is common. What research says about calling, passion and meaning.
  6. Feeling that you are wasting your time: The feeling that life is slipping away between chores and screens is very common. What causes it and how to reclaim the time that matters.
  7. Suicidal thoughts: Thinking about dying is more common than people think, and it can be talked about. What we know, which signs matter and how to ask for or offer help.
  8. Social isolation: Having few contacts or feeling lonely affects health as much as other well-known risk factors. How people get there and how to get out.
  9. Not fitting in: Feeling out of place in a country, a job or a group really hurts. Why belonging matters so much and how to build it.
  10. Heartbreak: A break-up or unrequited love can hurt like a physical wound. What happens in the mind and what helps you move on.
  11. Grieving the death of a family member: Losing a parent, partner or sibling changes your life. What is normal in grief, what helps and when to seek support.
  12. Facing the death of a child: It is one of the most devastating losses there is. What parents and siblings go through, what not to say and what can help.
  13. Receiving a serious diagnosis: Cancer, heart disease or a neurological illness: what tends to happen inside after the news and what helps you cope.
  14. Living with chronic pain or illness: When pain or illness stays: how it affects mood, why the mind influences pain and what helps you live better.
  15. School bullying: What it is and is not, how to spot it, why it leaves a mark and what families and schools can do.
  16. Workplace bullying: When work becomes a hostile place: how to tell bullying from conflict, what effects it has and how to protect yourself.
  17. Infertility: A silent grief that affects one in six people: its emotional impact, myths about stress and what helps couples.
  18. Caring for someone with dementia: Watching a loved one change: carer strain, how to communicate better and what support exists.
  19. Loneliness in old age: When friends are gone and days grow long: what the data say, why it matters for health and what helps.
  20. Intimate partner violence: It does not always leave visible marks: how to recognise control and abuse, why it is hard to leave and how to help someone.
If you are thinking of ending your life or are worried about someone, ask for help now. In the UK and Ireland you can call Samaritans on 116 123; in the United States and Canada, call or text 988; in Spain, call 024. In an emergency, call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department. Talking to someone you trust is a first step. More helplines.

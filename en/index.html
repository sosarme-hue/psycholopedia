Worrying can help us anticipate problems, but it can also become a loop that solves nothing.

Arguing without losing the conversation

Couples argue. What research looks at is not whether they argue but how they do it and how they repair afterwards.

Study more or study better?

Rereading and highlighting feel effective but are not very. Two strategies with strong support: testing yourself and spacing your study.

Our attention in a world of screens

Switching tasks has a cost. What is known about interruptions, notifications and multitasking, and what is still debated.

Adolescence: autonomy and bonds

More than a rebellious phase, adolescence is a period of intense social learning, with a changing brain and changing sleep.

Why do I get angry over little things?

Sometimes the spark is small, but the fuel had been building up for hours: hunger, sleep loss, hurry or worries.

Read →Emotions

What is sadness for?

Nobody wants to feel sad, but sadness serves useful purposes. Telling it apart from depression matters.

Read →Emotions

Why do I feel anxious for no obvious reason?

Sometimes anxiety comes out of nowhere. Understanding how it feeds itself helps it lose its grip.

Read →Emotions

Is it bad to be bored?

Boredom is uncomfortable, but it is a useful signal: it tells us what we are doing is not fulfilling.

Read →Self-esteem and identity

Why do I compare myself with others so much?

Comparing ourselves is almost unavoidable: it is one of the ways we gauge how we are doing. The problem is with whom and with what.

Read →Self-esteem and identity

Self-compassion: treating yourself like a good friend

Being hard on yourself does not usually motivate you more. Being kind to yourself after mistakes helps you improve.

Read →Self-esteem and identity

Why is it so hard to say no?

For fear of disappointing others, we agree to things we do not want. Saying no respectfully is a skill that can be learned.

Read →Self-esteem and identity

Perfectionism: when nothing is ever enough

Wanting to do things well is good. Demanding perfection and punishing yourself for every slip has costs.

Can passion last in a long relationship?

Intense infatuation tends to calm down, but some couples keep romantic love alive for decades.

Read →Couples

Jealousy: when does it stop being normal?

Feeling jealous now and then is human. Controlling, monitoring or isolating a partner is not.

Read →Couples

Getting over a breakup

A breakup really hurts, but most people recover, and some come out with a clearer sense of who they are.

Why do siblings fight so much?

Sibling fights are normal and can teach negotiation. What worsens them most is the sense of unequal treatment.

Read →Family

Tantrums: what to do when a child explodes

Tantrums are normal between one and four years old. Staying calm and not giving in to screaming helps most.

Read →Family

Caring for an older relative without losing yourself

Caring for a dependent parent is an act of love and also a burden. Looking after the carer is not selfish.

Read →Family

Does birth order shape personality?

The responsible eldest, the overlooked middle child, the rebellious youngest… Large studies find barely any differences.

Read →Friendship and social life

Why is it so hard to make friends as an adult?

At school, friendship arose almost by itself. As adults we lack what feeds it most: shared time and repeated encounters.

Read →Friendship and social life

Shyness and social anxiety: when other people are frightening

Being shy is a trait; social anxiety limits your life. We believe others watch us far more than they do.

Read →Friendship and social life

How to support someone going through a hard time

You often do not need the perfect words. Listening, validating and offering concrete help is usually worth more than advice.

Read →Friendship and social life

Is it worth talking to strangers?

We think it will be awkward and the other person will not want to. Experiments show both sides come out ahead.

Read →Habits

Why do New Year's resolutions fail?

It is not a lack of willpower. The way a goal is framed has a big influence on whether it is met.

Read →Habits

Does exercise really improve your mood?

Yes, and you do not need to run marathons. Brisk walking or yoga already has measurable effects.

Read →Habits

Nail-biting and other hard-to-break habits

Nail-biting, hair-pulling or skin-picking are usually automatic. A simple technique helps break them.

Read →Habits

Why do we eat when we are not hungry?

Eating out of boredom, stress or sadness is very common. Very strict diets sometimes make it worse.

Exam nerves

Some nerves help you perform. When they block you, two simple techniques have shown an effect: reframing them and writing about them.

Read →Study

Does studying with music help?

It depends on the task and the music. Songs with lyrics tend to hurt reading; soft music sometimes does no harm.

Read →Study

Is it too late to learn a language as an adult?

Children have the edge in sounding native, but adults learn fast at first and can reach a very high level.

Read →Work

Public speaking without dying of fright

It is one of the most common fears. Knowing that nerves show less than we think already helps.

Read →Work

Remote work: benefits and pitfalls

It saves commuting and gives flexibility, but can isolate you and blur work-life boundaries. Hybrid models fare well.

Read →Work

Why is it hard to switch off from work?

Thinking about work after you get home prevents recovery. Switching off is not laziness: it is part of performance.

Read →Work

Signs that work is burning you out

Exhaustion, cynicism and a sense of being useless. Spotting it early lets you act before you crash.

Read →Sleep

How many hours of sleep do you need?

Adults need between seven and nine hours, but regularity matters as much as quantity.

Read →Sleep

I wake up in the night and can't get back to sleep

Waking at night is normal. What prolongs wakefulness is worrying about not sleeping.

Read →Sleep

Are naps good for you?

A short nap improves alertness and mood. Long ones can leave you groggy and steal sleep at night.

Read →Sleep

Why do I have nightmares so often?

Stress and trauma make them more frequent. A simple technique, rewriting the dream while awake, reduces them.

Read →Sexuality

I don't feel like having sex: is something wrong with me?

Desire does not always strike out of the blue. In many people it appears after things begin, in response to intimacy.

Read →Sexuality

How to talk to your children about sex

Early, little by little and naturally is best. Talking about it does not bring sex forward; it makes it safer.

Read →Loss and transitions

Does grief have stages?

The five stages are a popular but inaccurate idea. Most people cope with loss better than they expect.

Read →Loss and transitions

Retirement: rest or emptiness?

Most people adapt well, but not everyone equally. Those who retire with plans and relationships fare better.

Read →Loss and transitions

Emigrating: the grief of leaving your country

Moving country means many losses at once. Keeping your own culture while opening up to the new one usually works best.

Read →Loss and transitions

The empty nest: when children leave home

Sadness at first is normal, but many parents find their wellbeing and relationship improve after children leave.

Read →Digital life

Do social media make us unhappy?

The average effect is small, but it is not the same for everyone. How you use them matters more than how long.

Read →Digital life

I can't stop reading bad news

Endlessly scrolling through distressing news feels like control, but it increases stress. Limiting it helps.

Read →Digital life

Video games: when should you worry?

Playing a lot is not an addiction. The worry is losing control and the game crowding out everything else.

Read →Decisions

I find it really hard to make decisions

Having many options can paralyse you. Settling for "good enough" usually brings more satisfaction.

Read →Decisions

Should you trust your gut?

Intuition works well where you have lots of practice and quick feedback. Outside those areas, it misleads.

Read →Decisions

Why do I buy things I don't need?

Impulse purchases respond to emotions and to an environment designed to tempt us. Small pauses help a lot.

Read →Decisions

What do we regret most?

In the short term, what we did. In the long term, what we did not dare to do.

Read →Culture and society

Why do we believe fake news?

More through inattention than gullibility. Stopping to consider whether something is true before sharing greatly reduces its spread.

Read →Culture and society

Eco-anxiety: worrying about the climate

Worrying about climate change is a reasonable reaction, not a disorder. Acting with others helps you cope.

Read →Lifespan

Does the midlife crisis exist?

Wellbeing seems to dip somewhat in midlife, but the dramatic crisis of the cliché is uncommon.

Ageing well

What changes and what does not in the mind with age, how to look after memory, mood and relationships, and when to seek help.

Lesson 3. Looking after the mind

1 · Lesson

The Lancet commission on dementia estimates that a substantial share of cases, close to half in its 2024 report, may be linked to risk factors that can be modified across life. It does not mean every case can be prevented, but that acting on them lowers risk across the population.

These factors include little schooling, untreated hearing or vision loss, high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol, smoking, excessive alcohol, physical inactivity, obesity, depression, social isolation, head injuries and air pollution.

In practice: move every day, look after blood pressure and blood sugar, do not smoke, get your hearing checked (and use hearing aids if needed), keep your mind active with activities you enjoy and, above all, stay in touch with other people. What is good for the heart is usually good for the brain.

2 · Example

Untreated hearing loss is one of the risk factors carrying most weight across the population. As well as reducing brain stimulation, it isolates: people who cannot hear well stop going to gatherings and conversations. A hearing aid can be a mental health measure.

3 · Activity

Go through the list of risk factors and mark those you or someone close could improve. Pick one and write a concrete step for next month.

Lesson 4. Mood, losses and meaning in later life

1 · Lesson

Old age brings losses: retirement, the death of a partner or friends, health problems, changes in independence. Feeling sad about them is normal. But depression is not an inevitable consequence of age, and it can be treated.

In older people depression can look different: more physical complaints, tiredness, loss of interest, irritability or memory problems mistaken for decline. It is often underdiagnosed because it is put down to "just age". In older men, suicide risk is especially high.

What protects: keeping meaningful roles (caring, teaching, taking part, volunteering), trusted relationships, physical and social activity, and not becoming isolated. Reminiscence, recalling and telling one's own story, can help make sense of it and pass it on to other generations.

2 · Example

A recently retired man may find that, without the routine and relationships of work, he spends his days at home and fades. Finding an activity with a schedule and other people, such as a community garden, a choir or volunteering, usually helps more than "resting" indefinitely.

3 · Activity

Ask an older person you know to tell you about an important moment in their life. Listen without hurrying and, if they agree, write it down. Afterwards, ask how it felt to tell it.

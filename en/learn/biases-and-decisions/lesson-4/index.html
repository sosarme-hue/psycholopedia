Lesson 4. Deciding a little better

1 · Lesson

Knowing about biases does little on its own: even people who study them keep falling into them. What works best is changing the decision procedure, especially for important decisions.

Some useful techniques: write down the options and criteria before looking at details; ask someone to play devil's advocate; run a "pre-mortem" (imagine the decision went wrong and ask why); use data from similar cases (the "outside view") rather than relying only on our own case; and sleep on it before deciding something emotionally charged.

In everyday life, not everything needs optimising. Barry Schwartz described how always seeking the best possible option can bring more dissatisfaction than settling for a "good enough" one. Saving System 2 for what matters is itself a good decision.

2 · Example

When we estimate how long a home renovation will take, we tend to focus on our own plan and underestimate (the planning fallacy). Asking how long similar renovations took gives far more realistic estimates.

3 · Activity

Pick a decision you have pending. Run a pre-mortem: imagine that a year from now it went wrong and write three possible reasons. Can you prevent any of them now?

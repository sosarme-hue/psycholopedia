Course 17

Brain and behaviour

Neurons, brain regions, plasticity and what neuroscience can and cannot tell us.

Lesson 1. Neurons and synapses

1 · Lesson

The human brain has about 86 billion neurons, plus a similar number of glial cells that support, nourish and protect them. Each neuron receives signals through its dendrites, integrates them and, if they pass a threshold, sends an electrical impulse along its axon.

Neurons communicate at synapses, tiny gaps where the electrical signal becomes chemical: neurotransmitters are released and act on the next neuron. Some are excitatory (such as glutamate) and others inhibitory (such as GABA). Others, such as dopamine or serotonin, modulate whole circuits.

Many drugs and substances act at synapses. But be wary of phrases like "depression is a lack of serotonin" or "dopamine is the pleasure molecule": each neurotransmitter takes part in many functions, and behaviour arises from networks, not from a single substance.

2 · Example

Dopamine has more to do with anticipation and learning what is worth pursuing than with pleasure itself. In experiments with monkeys, dopamine neurons fired at an unexpected reward and, with learning, began to fire at the cue that predicted it.

3 · Activity

Find a phrase about dopamine, serotonin or "happiness hormones" in a news story or an advert. Write down what it claims and whether it seems an oversimplification after this lesson.

Lesson 4. Practices that help and overblown promises

1 · Lesson

Positive psychology has studied simple interventions: writing down things you are grateful for, doing acts of kindness, using your strengths in new ways or imagining your best possible self. Pooled analyses show real but small effects, clearer in people who choose them and practise regularly.

Other well-supported activities are physical exercise, contact with nature, good sleep and spending time on absorbing activities (what Mihály Csíkszentmihályi called flow).

Be wary of promises of guaranteed happiness and of "toxic positivity", which treats any negative emotion as a failure. Unpleasant emotions are part of life and serve functions. And wellbeing does not depend only on what each person does: living and working conditions weigh heavily.

2 · Example

Flow appears when the challenge of a task matches our skills: if it is too easy we get bored, and if it is too hard we get overwhelmed. A musician, a surgeon or someone doing a crossword can lose track of time for the same reason.

3 · Activity

For a week, before going to sleep, write down three specific things that went well that day and why. At the end, judge whether it felt useful or forced. Both answers are valid.

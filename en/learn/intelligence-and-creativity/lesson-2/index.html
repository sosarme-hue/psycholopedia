Lesson 2. How it is measured

1 · Lesson

Early in the 20th century Alfred Binet created the first modern test to identify children who needed school support. Today the most widely used tests, such as the Wechsler scales, assess verbal and non-verbal reasoning, working memory and processing speed.

IQ is not an absolute quantity but a position compared with people of the same age. It is scaled so the mean is 100 and the standard deviation 15: about two in three people score between 85 and 115. A test given by a professional is very different from online "IQ tests".

IQ moderately predicts school and job performance, but leaves much unexplained: motivation, opportunity, personality and health all matter. Moreover, during the 20th century scores rose several points per decade in many countries (the Flynn effect), too fast to be due to genetics.

2 · Example

If someone today took an IQ test using 1950s norms, they would score considerably higher than with current norms. That is why tests are re-normed from time to time.

3 · Activity

Find an online "IQ test" and look at how many questions it has, whether it says what group it was compared with and who made it. Compare it with what you have read about professional tests.

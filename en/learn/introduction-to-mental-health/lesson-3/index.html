Lesson 3. Treatments and professionals

1 · Lesson

There are several types of professional. The family doctor is usually the way in and treats many common problems. The clinical psychologist and the health psychologist assess and provide psychotherapy. The psychiatrist is a specialist doctor who can prescribe medication. Mental health nursing, social work and occupational therapy complete the teams.

Treatments with support include psychotherapies (cognitive-behavioural, interpersonal, systemic, brief psychodynamic and others, depending on the problem), medication when indicated, and social and rehabilitation interventions. They are often combined.

The effectiveness of psychotherapy depends as much on technique as on the therapeutic alliance: the collaboration and trust between the person and the professional. It is reasonable to ask the professional which approach they use, how long it usually lasts and how they will judge whether it is working.

2 · Example

A person with panic attacks sees their family doctor, who rules out physical causes and refers them to psychology. After some sessions of cognitive-behavioural therapy with exposure, the attacks decrease. If needed, the doctor or psychiatrist could also consider medication.

3 · Activity

Find out how to access mental health care in your area: do you need to go through your family doctor? Are there waiting lists? What free resources exist (associations, helplines)? Write it down: it is useful information for you or someone close.

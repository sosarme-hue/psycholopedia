Loss and grief

What grief is really like, why it does not follow fixed stages, how to support someone and when it needs professional help.

Lesson 3. Supporting someone who is grieving

1 · Lesson

When someone else is grieving we often feel we must say something that fixes the pain. There is no such phrase. What helps most is presence: being there, listening without hurry, letting them talk about the person who died, and tolerating silence and tears.

Some well-meant phrases tend to hurt: "they're in a better place", "time heals everything", "you have to be strong", "at least…". Simple phrases help more: "I'm so sorry", "I don't know what to say, but I'm here", "what were they like?", or simply naming the person who died.

Practical help is very valuable: bringing food, handling some paperwork, looking after the children for an afternoon. Offering it concretely ("I'll bring you dinner on Thursday") works better than "call me if you need anything". And support is needed weeks and months later too, when everyone else has gone back to their lives.

2 · Example

Many bereaved people say that what they appreciated most was not the words at the funeral, but the message someone sent on the birthday of the person who died, months later. Remembering significant dates is a simple way of offering support.

3 · Activity

If you know someone who lost a loved one this year, put a significant date (a birthday, an anniversary) in your calendar and send them a short message that day.

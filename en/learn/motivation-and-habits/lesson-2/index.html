Motivation and habits

What drives us, how habits form, how to set useful goals and what to do about procrastination.

Lesson 2. How habits form

1 · Lesson

A habit is a behaviour that, through repetition in the same context, is triggered almost automatically by a cue, without having to decide on it each time. This frees attention for other things, and explains why habits are so useful and so hard to change.

Habit learning follows the principles of conditioning: a cue (a context, a time, an emotion), a behaviour and a consequence that reinforces it. With repetition, the behaviour comes to depend more on the cue than on the original goal.

How long does a habit take to form? The "21 days" figure has no basis. A study by Phillippa Lally and colleagues found an average of about two months, with huge variation: from a few weeks to almost a year, depending on the person and the behaviour. Missing the odd day did not ruin the process.

2 · Example

Many people who move house or change jobs find it easier to start cycling or drop an unhealthy habit. When the context changes, the cues that triggered the old behaviour disappear, opening a window to create new ones.

3 · Activity

Pick a habit you have and break it down: what is the cue, the behaviour and the reward? Do it for one you want to keep and one you want to change.

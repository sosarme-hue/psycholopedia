Parenting and family

Attachment, parenting styles, limits and emotions in the family, with what research says and no magic recipes.

Lesson 1. Attachment

1 · Lesson

John Bowlby proposed that babies are born with a tendency to seek closeness to their carers, especially when frightened or distressed. That bond, attachment, gives them a secure base from which to explore the world.

Mary Ainsworth observed how one-year-olds reacted to separation from and reunion with their mother in a laboratory setting. She described secure attachment (the child protests and is comforted on reunion) and several insecure patterns. Secure attachment is linked above all to sensitive carers, who notice the child's signals and respond appropriately most of the time.

There is no need to be perfect: being good enough is enough, and repairing when something goes wrong. Attachment is not fixed for ever in the first months; later relationships count too, and children can form secure bonds with several people.

2 · Example

Edward Tronick's "still face" studies showed that when a mother stops responding and keeps a blank face, the baby tries to regain her attention and quickly becomes distressed. What matters is not that mismatches never happen, which they constantly do, but that they are repaired.

3 · Activity

Think of a person you felt safe with as a child. Write down what they did that made you feel safe. Do you do something similar for someone today?

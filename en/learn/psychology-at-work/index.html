Psychology at work

What makes work healthy, burnout, teams, leadership and balance with personal life.

Lesson 1. What makes work healthy

1 · Lesson

Work can be a source of identity, relationships, structure and meaning, and also of stress and illness. Having good-quality employment is usually good for mental health; unemployment, by contrast, is linked to poorer wellbeing.

Robert Karasek proposed the demand–control model: the most stressful work combines high demands with little control over how to do it. Johannes Siegrist added effort–reward imbalance: a lot of effort with little recognition, pay or security also wears people down. Support from colleagues and managers buffers both.

Other important psychosocial factors are role clarity, fair treatment, workload, working hours and the absence of harassment. Many depend on the organisation, not just the individual: that is why preventing psychosocial risks is employers' responsibility.

2 · Example

An assembly-line job with an imposed pace and no breaks of the worker's choosing can be more stressful than that of a manager with a lot of responsibility but room to organise their time. Lack of control weighs as much as workload.

3 · Activity

Rate your current (or last) job from 1 to 5 on demands, control, reward and support. Which factor is lowest? Is there something small you could negotiate to improve it?

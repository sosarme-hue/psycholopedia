Lesson 1. What does psychology study?

1 · Lesson

Psychology is the science that studies behaviour and mental processes: how we perceive, learn, remember, feel, think, relate to others and change across our lives. The word comes from the Greek psyché (breath, soul) and lógos (study).

Three features set it apart from common sense: it asks questions that can be tested, it gathers data systematically and it revises its conclusions when new evidence appears. That is why it sometimes confirms what we suspected and sometimes surprises us.

It is not the same as psychiatry: psychiatry is a medical specialty; psychology is a discipline of its own, with basic branches (memory, perception, development…) and applied ones (clinical, educational, occupational, legal, sport…).

2 · Example

"Opposites attract" and "birds of a feather flock together" are two sayings that claim the opposite. Which is true? Social psychology has studied it: in general, partners and friends tend to resemble each other in values, attitudes and interests more than they differ. Common sense offers answers for everything; research helps choose between them.

3 · Activity

Think of a popular belief about the mind ("classical music makes babies smarter", "childhood memories are accurate", "people are left-brained or right-brained"). Write down: how could you test whether it is true? What would you compare? Whom would you ask?

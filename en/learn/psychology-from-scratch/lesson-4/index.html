Lesson 4. How psychology does research

1 · Lesson

Psychology uses several methods, and each answers a type of question. Observation describes what happens (how children play in a playground). Surveys collect what people say about themselves. Correlational studies measure whether two things go together (hours of sleep and grades). Experiments change a single thing and compare randomly assigned groups to find out whether that thing causes an effect.

Only a well-designed experiment allows us to speak of cause. Two things going together does not mean one causes the other: a third variable may explain both, or the relationship may run the other way.

A good study also looks after its sample (who was studied?), its measures (does it measure what it claims to?), its ethics (consent, confidentiality, doing no harm) and replication: a result gains confidence when other teams repeat it.

2 · Example

A study finds that children who eat breakfast get better grades. Does breakfast improve performance? We do not know: families who eat breakfast together may also have more regular routines, more sleep or more resources. To find out, we would need to compare groups that are alike in everything except breakfast.

3 · Activity

Find a news headline saying that something "causes", "triggers" or "improves" something related to the mind. Ask yourself: was it an experiment or a correlation? How many people were studied? What other explanation could there be?

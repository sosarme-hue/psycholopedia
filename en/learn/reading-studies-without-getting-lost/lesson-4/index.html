Lesson 4. Replication and open science

1 · Lesson

In 2015, a large collaborative project (the Open Science Collaboration) tried to repeat 100 studies published in psychology journals. Roughly a third of the replications obtained significant results in the same direction, and effects were generally smaller than the originals. It was one of the triggers of what became known as the replication crisis.

The causes were partly common practices: small samples, analysing data in many ways until something significant turned up, publishing only "positive" results. The response has been open science: preregistering hypotheses and analyses before collecting data, sharing data and materials, publishing null results too and running large-scale replications.

For reading a study, this translates into simple questions: has it been replicated? Was it preregistered? Is it a single study, or are there systematic reviews and meta-analyses bringing many studies together?

2 · Example

"Ego depletion" (the idea that willpower gets used up like a muscle) was supported by dozens of studies. A coordinated replication across many labs, published in 2016, found an effect close to zero. Today the idea is considered much more doubtful than it seemed.

3 · Activity

Choose a famous psychological finding you know. Look for a meta-analysis or a large-scale replication. Has the result held up, shrunk or disappeared?

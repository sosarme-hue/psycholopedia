Sport psychology

Motivation, focus, pressure and the mental benefits of exercise, for athletes and for anyone who just wants to move more.

Lesson 3. Motivation, confidence and injury

1 · Lesson

Sport distinguishes task orientation (focusing on improving and mastering skills) from ego orientation (focusing on beating others). Training climates that value effort and improvement are linked to more enjoyment, more persistence and less dropout, especially in children and adolescents.

Confidence is built mainly through experiences of success, but also by seeing similar others succeed, through encouragement from credible people and by learning to interpret bodily sensations. Visualisation or imagery practice, mentally rehearsing performance, can improve performance when combined with real practice.

An injury is not just a physical problem: it can bring frustration, fear of re-injury, loss of identity and isolation from the team. Psychological support during recovery helps people stick to rehabilitation and return with confidence.

2 · Example

In children's sport, one of the most cited reasons for quitting is that "it's not fun any more". Coaches who praise effort and individual improvement, rather than just results, usually keep more children playing for longer.

3 · Activity

Before your next exercise session, spend two minutes imagining in detail doing it well: movements, sensations, surroundings. Afterwards, note whether you noticed any difference.

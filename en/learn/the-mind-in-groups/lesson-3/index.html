The mind in groups

Social psychology: how others influence us, why we obey, how prejudice arises and when we help.

Lesson 3. Stereotypes and prejudice

1 · Lesson

A stereotype is a generalised belief about members of a group; prejudice, an attitude (usually negative) towards them; and discrimination, behaviour that treats them differently because they belong to that group.

Categorising is something the mind does naturally to simplify the world, and simply dividing people into two arbitrary groups is enough for them to start favouring their own, as Henri Tajfel showed. Social identity theory explains that part of our self-esteem comes from the groups we belong to.

Among the things that most reduce prejudice is contact between groups under favourable conditions: equal status, common goals, cooperation and institutional support, as Gordon Allport proposed. Reviews of hundreds of studies confirm that contact tends to reduce prejudice.

2 · Example

In Tajfel's minimal group experiments, participants were assigned to a group on a trivial basis, such as preferring a Klee or a Kandinsky painting. Even so, when sharing out rewards, they tended to favour their own group, even at the cost of everyone getting less.

3 · Activity

Write down a stereotype you have heard about a group you belong to. Does it describe you? Now think of a group you have little contact with. What do you know about its members from direct experience, and what from hearsay?

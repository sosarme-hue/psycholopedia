Understanding emotions

Concepts, context and emotional vocabulary.

Lesson 1. What an emotion is

1 · Lesson

An emotion is a brief, organised response to something that matters to us. It has several components occurring together: an appraisal of the situation ("this is a threat", "I have lost something"), bodily changes (heart, breathing, tension), an action tendency (flee, approach, attack), an expression (face, voice, posture) and a subjective experience (what we feel).

It helps to distinguish emotion, feeling and mood. An emotion is intense and short, and usually has a clear object. A mood is more diffuse and lasting: we can be in a bad mood without quite knowing why.

Emotions have a function. Fear prepares us to protect ourselves; sadness invites us to stop and seek support; anger signals that something is perceived as unfair; joy reinforces what does us good. There are no "bad" emotions: there are responses that help us in some contexts and complicate life in others.

2 · Example

You are walking down the street and hear brakes screech. In one second: your heart races, you turn your head, step back and feel a jolt of fear. When you see nothing has happened, your body takes a few minutes to calm down. All the components of the emotion acted together and very fast.

3 · Activity

Remember the last time you felt a strong emotion. Describe it with the five components: what did you think? What did you notice in your body? What did it urge you to do? How did it show? How did you experience it?

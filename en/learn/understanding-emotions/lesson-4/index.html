Lesson 4. Regulating emotions

1 · Lesson

Regulating an emotion is not eliminating it but influencing when it appears, how long it lasts and how it is expressed. James Gross proposed a model that orders strategies by when they act: choosing the situation, modifying it, shifting attention, changing how we interpret it (reappraisal) or modifying the response once under way (for example, suppressing its expression).

Reappraisal is usually associated with better outcomes than suppression: reinterpreting a situation reduces the emotion, while hiding it keeps internal arousal going and can affect relationships. But no strategy is good everywhere: what helps is flexibility and choosing according to context.

Basic things also help: sleeping, moving, talking to someone you trust, breathing slowly. When an emotion is so intense or persistent that it interferes with daily life, it is worth seeking professional help.

2 · Example

Before a job interview your heart is pounding. You can try not to think about it (it usually works poorly), or reinterpret it: "my body is getting ready to perform". Some studies have found that interpreting arousal as useful energy improves performance in evaluative situations.

3 · Activity

Remember a recent situation that angered or worried you. Write down three different ways of interpreting it. Does the emotion change with each interpretation? Which seems most accurate to the facts?

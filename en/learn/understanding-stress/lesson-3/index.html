Understanding stress

What stress is, what it does to the body, how we appraise it and which strategies work to manage it.

Lesson 3. How we appraise what happens

1 · Lesson

Richard Lazarus and Susan Folkman proposed that stress arises from appraisal. In primary appraisal we ask whether the situation is irrelevant, positive or threatening. In secondary appraisal, whether we have the resources to cope. Stress appears when demand seems greater than resources.

This explains why a sense of control and predictability matter so much. A hard task with room to decide how to do it is less stressful than a similar task where nothing can be decided. The same goes for prolonged uncertainty, which often wears us down more than a definite piece of bad news.

Social support is one of the most protective resources: having someone to talk to, who helps with practical things or simply keeps us company, reduces the impact of stressors on body and mood.

2 · Example

Waiting days for a medical test result is often more distressing than getting the diagnosis. With the result in hand, even if it is not good, you can plan; while waiting, the mind circles all the possibilities at once.

3 · Activity

Pick something that worries you now. Split a sheet in two: what depends on you and what does not. Write a small first step for something in the first column.

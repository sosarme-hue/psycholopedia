Personality disorders and related traits

CIE-11: block 6D10–6D11 · look up in ICD-11 ↗
DSM-5-TR: Personality disorders

They describe persistent ways of seeing oneself, relating to others and regulating emotions that cause significant and lasting problems in different areas of life.

This is where the two classifications differ most. DSM-5-TR keeps ten types (paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal, antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic, avoidant, dependent and obsessive-compulsive), arranged in three clusters, although it proposes an alternative dimensional model. ICD-11 has abandoned types: it first assesses the severity of the problem (mild, moderate or severe) and then describes which traits characterise it, with the option of specifying a borderline pattern. This change aims to better reflect research showing that personality varies continuously.

What it includes

  • Mild, moderate or severe personality disorder
  • Prominent traits or patterns: negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition, anankastia and borderline pattern

Neurodevelopment: ADHD, autism and learning difficulties

Differences in brain development that appear in childhood: how they are detected and which supports help.

Medical review Alejandro Rosàs, family doctor

Need help now? In Spain, 024 is free and open 24 hours; in an emergency call 112. Elsewhere, contact your local helpline or emergency services. This text is for general information: it cannot diagnose and does not replace seeing a professional.

What they are

Neurodevelopmental disorders are conditions that show up in childhood and affect how functions such as attention, communication, learning or motor skills develop. Many people prefer to speak of neurodiversity to stress that these are different ways of functioning, with difficulties and also strengths.

ADHD

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder involves persistent difficulties with attention, hyperactivity or impulsivity, present before age 12 and in several settings (home, school, work). It affects around 5% of children and often continues into adulthood, frequently with less visible hyperactivity and more disorganisation.

Autism

Autism spectrum disorder involves differences in social communication and interaction and restricted or repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests or activities, often with over- or under-sensitivity to sounds, textures or lights. It is a very broad spectrum: from people who need a lot of support to others who are highly independent. It is diagnosed in around 1% of children, a figure that has risen mainly because detection has improved.

Specific learning difficulties

Such as dyslexia (reading), dyscalculia (maths) or dysgraphia (writing): persistent difficulties with a particular skill that are not explained by intelligence or lack of opportunity.

What they are not

They are not the result of poor parenting or lack of effort. ADHD is not "a lively child" and autism is not caused by lack of affection. Vaccines do not cause autism: studies of millions of children have ruled it out, and the 1998 paper that suggested it was retracted for fraud.

How they are assessed

With information from the family, school and direct observation, and specific tests carried out by experienced professionals (paediatrics, paediatric neurology, child psychiatry, clinical and educational psychology). Vision, hearing and sleep problems are ruled out. In Spain, school guidance teams and early intervention centres play a key role.

Which interventions are studied

  • ADHD: parent training in behaviour management, school adjustments, cognitive behavioural therapy for adolescents and adults and, when indicated, medication (methylphenidate, lisdexamfetamine, atomoxetine and others), which is effective and safe with monitoring.
  • Autism: early intervention focused on communication and interaction, involving the family; visual supports; adapting the sensory environment; support at school and work. No treatment "cures" autism, and therapies that promise to should be treated with suspicion.
  • Learning difficulties: explicit, systematic teaching of the affected skill, frequent practice and adjustments such as extra time in exams.

How to seek help

Share your concerns with the paediatrician or family doctor and with the school. An accurate diagnosis opens the door to support and adjustments.

How to support someone

Focus on strengths as well as difficulties. Offer structure, clear instructions and advance warning of changes. Listen to neurodivergent adults: their experience helps us understand which supports work.

Myths to avoid

  • "ADHD was invented to medicate children". It is one of the most studied disorders, though there is debate about overdiagnosis in some places and underdiagnosis in others, especially in girls.
  • "Autistic people lack empathy". They may find social cues hard to read, but they do not lack the capacity to care about others.
  • "Dyslexia is seeing letters backwards". It is mainly a difficulty linking sounds and letters.

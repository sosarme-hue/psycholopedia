Personality disorders

Persistent patterns that cause suffering: what they mean, borderline personality disorder and effective treatments.

Medical review Alejandro Rosàs, family doctor

Need help now? In Spain, 024 is free and open 24 hours; in an emergency call 112. Elsewhere, contact your local helpline or emergency services. This text is for general information: it cannot diagnose and does not replace seeing a professional. Where to get help →

What they are

We speak of a personality disorder when a way of thinking, feeling, relating to others and controlling impulses is persistent, appears in many situations, departs from what is expected and causes significant suffering or problems for the person or those around them.

The World Health Organization's ICD-11 dropped the old categories and proposes rating severity (mild, moderate, severe) and describing the predominant traits: negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition and anankastia (rigidity and perfectionism), with a specifier for a borderline pattern. DSM-5 keeps ten classic categories, such as borderline, antisocial, narcissistic, avoidant and obsessive-compulsive personality disorder.

Borderline personality disorder

It is the most studied and one of the most commonly seen in clinics. It is marked by very intense, shifting emotions, fear of abandonment, unstable relationships, impulsivity, a sense of emptiness and, often, self-harm or suicide attempts. It is usually linked to early emotional vulnerability combined with adverse experiences.

What they are not

They are not being "a bad person" or a moral defect. Nor are they unchangeable: follow-up studies show many symptoms improve over the years. Having marked traits, such as being a perfectionist or distrustful, does not mean having a disorder unless it causes a significant problem.

How they are assessed

Through in-depth clinical interviews, often several, information from people close to the person and questionnaires. Diagnosis should be made cautiously, especially in adolescents, and without letting the label be used in a stigmatising way.

Which interventions are studied

  • Dialectical behaviour therapy (Marsha Linehan): combines acceptance and change, with skills training in emotion regulation, distress tolerance, interpersonal effectiveness and mindfulness. It reduces self-harm.
  • Mentalization-based treatment, schema therapy and transference-focused psychotherapy also have support.
  • No medicines are specifically approved for personality disorders; sometimes they are used for particular symptoms.
  • Good coordination between professionals and a clear crisis plan are essential.

How to seek help

Through the family doctor or mental health services. For self-harm or suicidal thoughts, call 024 in Spain or your local crisis line, or emergency services if there is immediate danger.

How to support someone

Validate emotions without validating harmful behaviour ("I understand you're furious; it's not OK to insult me"). Keep consistent limits. Look after yourself and seek guidance: there are training programmes for families.

Myths to avoid

  • "They're manipulative". Many behaviours are desperate attempts to manage intense emotional pain.
  • "There's no treatment". There are psychotherapies with proven efficacy.
  • "It's for life". Most people with borderline personality disorder improve substantially over time.

Tests

57 tests used in psychology

Descriptive profiles of the most widely used assessment instruments: what they measure, who created them, how they are given and what their limits are.

This is a catalogue, not a consultation. Psycholopedia does not reproduce the items of these tests: many are copyrighted, and administering and interpreting them is a job for trained professionals. No test establishes a diagnosis on its own. Many have adapted, normed versions in different countries. The free-to-use ones can be taken and downloaded here.

Intelligence and development (6)

Measure general and specific cognitive abilities, or development in early childhood.

WAIS-IV · Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale
Authors
David Wechsler
Year
2008 (1.ª ed. 1955)
What it measures
General intelligence (full-scale IQ) and indices of verbal comprehension, perceptual reasoning, working memory and processing speed.
Format
Individually administered by a professional; ages 16 to 90; about 60–90 minutes.
Use and limits
Clinical, neuropsychological and forensic assessment. IQ is an estimate with a margin of error, not a fixed measure of the person.
WISC-V · Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children
Authors
David Wechsler
Year
2014 (1.ª ed. 1949)
What it measures
General intellectual ability and five indices: verbal, visuospatial, fluid reasoning, working memory and speed.
Format
Individual; ages 6 to 16.
Use and limits
Learning difficulties, giftedness and school assessment. Interpreted together with the child's history and context.
SB5 · Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scales
Authors
Gale Roid (sobre Binet y Terman)
Year
2003 (origen 1905/1916)
What it measures
Fluid reasoning, knowledge, quantitative reasoning, visuospatial processing and working memory.
Format
Individual; ages 2 to 85+.
Use and limits
Descendant of Binet and Simon's first intelligence test. Useful at the extremes of ability.
Raven · Raven's Progressive Matrices
Authors
John C. Raven
Year
1938
What it measures
Non-verbal abstract reasoning (completing figure series), closely related to fluid intelligence.
Format
Individual or group; versions for children, adults and advanced levels.
Use and limits
Used when language is a barrier. Even so, schooling and culture influence performance.
KABC-II · Kaufman Assessment Battery for Children
Authors
Alan y Nadeen Kaufman
Year
2004 (1.ª ed. 1983)
What it measures
Sequential and simultaneous processing, learning, planning and knowledge.
Format
Individual; ages 3 to 18.
Use and limits
Partly inspired by Luria's neuropsychology. Offers a score that reduces the weight of language.
Bayley-4 · Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development
Authors
Nancy Bayley
Year
2019 (1.ª ed. 1969)
What it measures
Cognitive, language and motor development in early childhood.
Format
Individual, through structured play; ages 1 to 42 months.
Use and limits
Follow-up of premature babies and detection of delays. Poorly predicts later intelligence in young infants.

Neuropsychology and cognitive screening (11)

Explore memory, attention, executive functions, language and visuospatial skills.

MMSE · Mini-Mental State Examination
Authors
Marshal Folstein, Susan Folstein y Paul McHugh
Year
1975
What it measures
Orientation, memory, attention, calculation, language and praxis, on 30 points.
Format
Clinician-administered; about 10 minutes.
Use and limits
Widely used screening for cognitive impairment in primary care. Insensitive to mild stages and strongly influenced by education.
MoCA · Montreal Cognitive Assessment
Authors
Ziad Nasreddine y colaboradores
Year
2005
What it measures
Attention, executive functions, memory, language, abstraction and orientation, on 30 points.
Format
Clinician-administered; about 10 minutes.
Use and limits
More sensitive than the MMSE to mild cognitive impairment. A low score calls for a full assessment, not a diagnosis.
Reloj · Clock Drawing Test
Authors
Varios autores (versiones de Shulman, Sunderland y otros)
Year
años 80
What it measures
Planning, visuospatial skills, comprehension and memory when drawing a clock showing a given time.
Format
Clinician-administered; 2–5 minutes.
Use and limits
Quick screening, often combined with other tests. There are several scoring systems.
TMT · Trail Making Test
Authors
Batería del Ejército de EE. UU.; difundido por Ralph Reitan
Year
1944
What it measures
Processing speed, attention and cognitive flexibility (part B: alternating numbers and letters).
Format
Pencil and paper; about 5 minutes.
Use and limits
Widely used in neuropsychology. Sensitive to many conditions, specific to none.
Stroop · Stroop Colour and Word Test
Authors
John Ridley Stroop (versión clínica de Charles Golden)
Year
1935 (1978)
What it measures
Inhibitory control: naming the ink colour while ignoring the written word.
Format
Individual; about 5 minutes.
Use and limits
One of the most replicated paradigms in psychology. You can try a popular version on Psycholopedia.
ROCF · Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure
Authors
André Rey y Paul-Alexandre Osterrieth
Year
1941–1944
What it measures
Visuospatial organisation, planning and visual memory: copying a complex figure and reproducing it from memory.
Format
Pencil and paper; individual.
Use and limits
A classic of child and adult neuropsychology.
WCST · Wisconsin Card Sorting Test
Authors
David Grant y Esta Berg
Year
1948
What it measures
Cognitive flexibility and concept formation: discovering and switching sorting rules.
Format
Individual; on paper or computer.
Use and limits
Associated with frontal executive functions, although other regions also affect performance.
RAVLT · Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test
Authors
André Rey
Year
1964
What it measures
Verbal learning and memory: recalling a 15-word list over several trials.
Format
Individual; with delayed recall and recognition.
Use and limits
Useful in assessing memory complaints and dementias.
BNT · Boston Naming Test
Authors
Edith Kaplan, Harold Goodglass y Sandra Weintraub
Year
1983
What it measures
Ability to name drawn objects (60 pictures).
Format
Individual.
Use and limits
Assessment of aphasia and dementia. Some pictures are culture-dependent and need adaptation.
WMS-IV · Wechsler Memory Scale
Authors
David Wechsler
Year
2009 (1.ª ed. 1945)
What it measures
Auditory, visual, immediate, delayed and working memory.
Format
Individual; ages 16 to 90.
Use and limits
Often used with the WAIS to compare memory with general ability.
d2 · d2 Test of Attention
Authors
Rolf Brickenkamp
Year
1962
What it measures
Selective attention and concentration: quickly crossing out certain letters among distractors.
Format
Individual or group; about 8 minutes.
Use and limits
Widely used in educational, traffic and personnel selection settings.

Personality (7)

Questionnaires describing personality traits and patterns.

MMPI-2 · Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory
Authors
Starke Hathaway y J. C. McKinley (revisión de Butcher y otros)
Year
1989 (1.ª ed. 1943)
What it measures
Clinical and personality profile, with validity scales that detect inconsistent or exaggerated responding.
Format
Self-report; 567 true/false items. MMPI-2-RF and MMPI-3 also exist.
Use and limits
One of the most researched in the world, in clinical and forensic settings.
NEO-PI-R · NEO Personality Inventory–Revised
Authors
Paul Costa y Robert McCrae
Year
1992
What it measures
The Big Five traits (neuroticism, extraversion, openness, agreeableness and conscientiousness) and 30 facets.
Format
Self-report; 240 items. Short version: NEO-FFI.
Use and limits
A reference in research on normal personality. Not designed to diagnose disorders.
BFI · Big Five Inventory
Authors
Oliver John y colaboradores
Year
1991 (BFI-2, 2017)
What it measures
The Big Five traits in brief form.
Format
Self-report; 44 items (60 in the BFI-2).
Use and limits
Free for research use and widely translated.
EPQ-R · Eysenck Personality Questionnaire–Revised
Authors
Hans y Sybil Eysenck
Year
1985
What it measures
Extraversion, neuroticism and psychoticism, plus a lie scale.
Format
Self-report; full and short versions.
Use and limits
Represents Eysenck's biologically based three-factor model.
16PF · Sixteen Personality Factor Questionnaire
Authors
Raymond Cattell
Year
1949 (5.ª ed. 1993)
What it measures
Sixteen primary traits and five global dimensions.
Format
Self-report; adults.
Use and limits
Widely used in counselling and personnel selection.
MCMI-IV · Millon Clinical Multiaxial Inventory
Authors
Theodore Millon y colaboradores
Year
2015 (1.ª ed. 1977)
What it measures
Personality patterns and clinical syndromes in clinical populations.
Format
Self-report; 195 items.
Use and limits
Designed for people in assessment or treatment, not the general population.
PAI · Personality Assessment Inventory
Authors
Leslie Morey
Year
1991
What it measures
Clinical, treatment, interpersonal and validity scales.
Format
Self-report; 344 items with four options.
Use and limits
An alternative to the MMPI in clinical and forensic settings.

Projective techniques (3)

Present ambiguous stimuli or open tasks. Their scientific validity is debated.

Rorschach · Rorschach Inkblot Test
Authors
Hermann Rorschach
Year
1921
What it measures
Perception and organisation of responses to 10 inkblots.
Format
Individual; coded with systems such as Exner's or R-PAS (2011).
Use and limits
Some variables have empirical support (for example, certain indicators of thought disturbance); many traditional interpretations do not.
TAT · Thematic Apperception Test
Authors
Christiana Morgan y Henry Murray
Year
1935
What it measures
Themes, motives and relationships from the stories the person makes up about pictures.
Format
Individual; without a single scoring system.
Use and limits
Inspired McClelland's measure of achievement motivation. Its clinical use is poorly standardised.
HTP · House-Tree-Person Test
Authors
John Buck
Year
1948
What it measures
Drawings of a house, a tree and a person, interpreted in relation to emotional life.
Format
Individual; pencil and paper; common with children.
Use and limits
Useful for starting a conversation. Research does not support interpreting specific traits from drawing details.

Symptoms and mental health in adults (17)

Screening and severity scales for problems such as depression, anxiety or alcohol use.

BDI-II · Beck Depression Inventory
Authors
Aaron Beck, Robert Steer y Gregory Brown
Year
1996 (1.ª ed. 1961)
What it measures
Severity of depressive symptoms over the past two weeks.
Format
Self-report; 21 items; from age 13.
Use and limits
Widely used to track change. Does not diagnose on its own.
PHQ-9 · Patient Health Questionnaire
Authors
Kurt Kroenke, Robert Spitzer y Janet Williams
Year
2001
What it measures
Depression symptoms based on diagnostic criteria, in 9 items.
Format
Self-report; about 3 minutes; free to use.
Use and limits
Standard in primary care. Its item 9 on thoughts of death always requires clinical follow-up.
HADS · Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale
Authors
Anthony Zigmond y Philip Snaith
Year
1983
What it measures
Anxiety and depression in people with physical illness, avoiding somatic symptoms.
Format
Self-report; 14 items.
Use and limits
Widely used in hospitals and medical research.
GAD-7 · Generalised Anxiety Disorder scale
Authors
Robert Spitzer y colaboradores
Year
2006
What it measures
Severity of generalised anxiety over the past two weeks.
Format
Self-report; 7 items; free to use.
Use and limits
Brief screening, also useful for other anxiety disorders.
STAI · State-Trait Anxiety Inventory
Authors
Charles Spielberger y colaboradores
Year
1970
What it measures
Anxiety as a state (how you feel now) and as a trait (how you usually feel).
Format
Self-report; 40 items.
Use and limits
A classic in stress and anxiety research.
BAI · Beck Anxiety Inventory
Authors
Aaron Beck y colaboradores
Year
1988
What it measures
Anxiety symptoms, with weight on physical symptoms.
Format
Self-report; 21 items.
Use and limits
Designed to distinguish anxiety from depression.
HAM-D · Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression
Authors
Max Hamilton
Year
1960
What it measures
Clinician-rated severity of depression.
Format
Clinician-administered by interview; 17 items in the classic version.
Use and limits
Widely used in antidepressant clinical trials.
Y-BOCS · Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale
Authors
Wayne Goodman y colaboradores
Year
1989
What it measures
Severity of obsessions and compulsions: time, interference, distress, resistance and control.
Format
Semi-structured interview; a child version (CY-BOCS) exists.
Use and limits
The standard for measuring OCD and its response to treatment.
PCL-5 · PTSD Checklist for DSM-5
Authors
Frank Weathers y colaboradores
Year
2013
What it measures
Post-traumatic stress symptoms over the past month.
Format
Self-report; 20 items; free to use.
Use and limits
Screening and monitoring; diagnosis requires an interview.
AUDIT · Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test
Authors
Organización Mundial de la Salud (Saunders y colaboradores)
Year
1993
What it measures
Hazardous use, harmful use and alcohol dependence.
Format
Self-report or interview; 10 items. Short version: AUDIT-C.
Use and limits
Recommended in primary care for brief interventions.
EAT-26 · Eating Attitudes Test
Authors
David Garner y colaboradores
Year
1982 (original 1979)
What it measures
Attitudes and behaviours that signal eating disorder risk.
Format
Self-report; 26 items.
Use and limits
Screening in general and school populations. Does not diagnose.
SCL-90-R · Symptom Checklist-90-Revised
Authors
Leonard Derogatis
Year
1977 (revisado después)
What it measures
Nine symptom dimensions (somatisation, anxiety, depression, hostility…) and a global index.
Format
Self-report; 90 items.
Use and limits
A general overview of psychological distress. Its dimensions overlap considerably.
PANSS · Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale
Authors
Stanley Kay, Abraham Fiszbein y Lewis Opler
Year
1987
What it measures
Positive, negative and general psychopathology symptoms in schizophrenia.
Format
Clinical interview; 30 items.
Use and limits
A reference in psychosis research and clinical trials.
YMRS · Young Mania Rating Scale
Authors
Robert Young y colaboradores
Year
1978
What it measures
Severity of manic symptoms.
Format
Clinician-rated; 11 items.
Use and limits
Used in monitoring bipolar disorder.
BHS · Beck Hopelessness Scale
Authors
Aaron Beck y colaboradores
Year
1974
What it measures
Negative expectations about the future.
Format
Self-report; 20 true/false items.
Use and limits
Hopelessness is associated with suicide risk, but no scale replaces clinical risk assessment.
GDS · Geriatric Depression Scale
Authors
Jerome Yesavage y colaboradores
Year
1982
What it measures
Depressive symptoms in older people, with few somatic items.
Format
Self-report; yes/no; 30 items or a 15-item short form.
Use and limits
Adapted to old age, where physical symptoms can mislead.
EPDS · Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale
Authors
John Cox, Jenifer Holden y Ruth Sagovsky
Year
1987
What it measures
Depressive symptoms during pregnancy and after childbirth.
Format
Self-report; 10 items.
Use and limits
Recommended in maternal health programmes.

Childhood and neurodevelopment (5)

Instruments for child behaviour, autism and ADHD.

M-CHAT-R/F · Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers
Authors
Diana Robins y colaboradores
Year
2001 (revisada 2014)
What it measures
Early signs of autism.
Format
Questionnaire for families; ages 16 to 30 months; follow-up interview.
Use and limits
Paediatric screening. A positive result leads to specialist assessment, not a diagnosis.
ADOS-2 · Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule
Authors
Catherine Lord y colaboradores
Year
2012 (1.ª ed. 2000)
What it measures
Communication, social interaction, play and repetitive behaviours through standardised activities.
Format
Observation by trained professionals; modules by age and language.
Use and limits
A reference tool, combined with developmental history (for example, the ADI-R interview).
SDQ · Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire
Authors
Robert Goodman
Year
1997
What it measures
Emotional problems, conduct, hyperactivity, peer relationships and prosocial behaviour.
Format
25 items; versions for parents, teachers and adolescents.
Use and limits
Brief, free for non-commercial use and translated into dozens of languages.
CBCL · Child Behavior Checklist
Authors
Thomas Achenbach
Year
1991 (sistema ASEBA, 2001)
What it measures
Internalising (anxiety, sadness) and externalising (aggression, rule-breaking) problems.
Format
Parent questionnaire; teacher and youth self-report versions.
Use and limits
One of the most used systems in child research worldwide.
ASRS · Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale
Authors
Organización Mundial de la Salud (Ronald Kessler y colaboradores)
Year
2005
What it measures
Inattention and hyperactivity-impulsivity symptoms in adults.
Format
Self-report; 18 items, with a 6-item screener.
Use and limits
Screening; diagnosis requires a childhood history and clinical assessment.

Well-being, stress and context (8)

Self-esteem, life satisfaction, stress, sleep, loneliness, carer burden and burnout.

RSES · Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale
Authors
Morris Rosenberg
Year
1965
What it measures
Global self-esteem.
Format
Self-report; 10 items.
Use and limits
Probably the most used self-esteem scale in research.
SWLS · Satisfaction With Life Scale
Authors
Ed Diener y colaboradores
Year
1985
What it measures
Global judgement of satisfaction with one's life.
Format
Self-report; 5 items.
Use and limits
Widely used in well-being studies and cross-country comparisons.
PSS · Perceived Stress Scale
Authors
Sheldon Cohen, Tom Kamarck y Robin Mermelstein
Year
1983
What it measures
The degree to which life situations are appraised as unpredictable, uncontrollable or overwhelming.
Format
Self-report; 14-, 10- and 4-item versions.
Use and limits
Measures the appraisal of stress, not the events themselves.
ISI · Insomnia Severity Index
Authors
Charles Morin
Year
1993
What it measures
Insomnia severity and its daytime impact.
Format
Self-report; 7 items.
Use and limits
Useful for screening and for measuring response to insomnia therapy.
UCLA · UCLA Loneliness Scale
Authors
Daniel Russell, Letitia Peplau y Mary Ferguson
Year
1978 (versión 3, 1996)
What it measures
Subjective feeling of loneliness.
Format
Self-report; 20 items; 3-item versions exist.
Use and limits
Widely used in studies of unwanted loneliness and health.
Zarit · Zarit Burden Interview
Authors
Steven Zarit y colaboradores
Year
1980
What it measures
Burden felt by someone caring for a dependent person.
Format
Self-report or interview; 22 items; short versions.
Use and limits
Widely used in primary care and social services.
WHO-5 · WHO-5 Well-Being Index
Authors
Organización Mundial de la Salud
Year
1998
What it measures
Psychological well-being over the past two weeks.
Format
Self-report; 5 items; free to use.
Use and limits
Positively worded; also used as a depression screen.
MBI · Maslach Burnout Inventory
Authors
Christina Maslach y Susan Jackson
Year
1981
What it measures
Emotional exhaustion, depersonalisation (cynicism) and personal accomplishment at work.
Format
Self-report; versions for health, education and general occupations.
Use and limits
The reference in the study of occupational burnout.
